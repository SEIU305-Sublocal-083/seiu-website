Oregon’s legal process

What makes a public employee strike lawful?

The Public Employee Collective Bargaining Act (PECBA) governs this process. For ordinary successor-contract bargaining, the Oregon Employment Relations Board describes these steps: [3]

  1. Direct bargaining. Ordinarily, 150 calendar days of good-faith bargaining precede a unilateral mediation request; both sides may agree to mediation sooner.
  2. Mediation. After at least 15 days, either side may declare impasse if there is no agreement.
  3. Final offers and cooling off. Final offers and cost summaries are due within seven days of impasse. Their publication starts the 30-day cooling-off period.
  4. Notice. A strike-permitted unit must give 10 days’ notice by certified mail to the employer and ERB, stating its intent and reasons. Notice may run during cooling off, but the strike cannot start before that period ends.

ORS 243.726 also requires an appropriate represented unit, good-faith compliance and a qualifying contract expiration or reopening. It excludes certain unconventional strike activity. [4]

Does every OSU worker have the same right to strike?

No. Our notice concerns strike-permitted classified workers in the SEIU Higher Education bargaining unit. It is not a strike call for every person at OSU. [1] Oregon law prohibits strikes in specified public safety and other units. Confirm your unit with a steward, especially if your job, appointment or employment status recently changed. [4]

Being represented by our contract, paying membership dues and qualifying for strike benefits are separate questions. Use our pay and benefits guide to identify what benefit administrators still need to confirm.

Protection and practical limits

If you are worried about retaliation

PECBA protects lawful union activity and bars employer interference, coercion and discrimination to encourage or discourage union membership. Report threats or adverse treatment tied to protected activity promptly so our stewards can assess the facts and response. [4]

Do not improvise an on-and-off strike. ORS 243.672(3) identifies sitdowns, slowdowns, rolling and intermittent strikes as prohibited unconventional activity. Follow our union’s instructions and ask before combining work and strike time. [4]

Keep picketing peaceful, respect access and safety instructions, and bring conflicts to a picket captain. A lawful strike does not turn threats, violence or property damage into approved strike activity. Questions about essential duties, probation, a second OSU job or return-to-work instructions need an individual answer from our organizer or legal team.

If management questions you or gives a direction

  1. Request representation when appropriate. Our posted agreement, Article 17, Section 5(A), provides representation on request during an investigatory interview you reasonably believe will result in discipline. Use the Weingarten rights guide and contact a steward. [5]
  2. Make a clear record. Note the date, exact words, witnesses and any direction. Save messages you are entitled to keep, and ask for directions in writing.
  3. Contact our stewards promptly. Do not wait for a situation to escalate. Ask about recording rules before recording a conversation and about any deadline that applies to your concern.
