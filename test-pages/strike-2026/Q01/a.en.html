Questions before the vote and a possible strike

Answers checked Saturday, Sept. 12, 2026. Find your question, then use the source or contact below it to take the next step. Where a rule is not verified, ask the named contact how it applies to you.

Voting and the ballot

Where and when can I vote? Monday, Sept. 14, 7:30 a.m.–5 p.m. Pacific, Memorial Union Multipurpose Room, 2501 SW Jefferson Way, Corvallis, OR 97331. The separate evening session is announced for 5–7:30 p.m. Pacific; confirm its address with Sylvia before traveling. MU daytime voting ends at 5 p.m. OSU-Cascades votes Tuesday, Sept. 15; ask Sylvia for the room and hours. No Zoom voting is established for this ballot.

How do I vote absentee? Use the personal request link in your own Friday, Sept. 11 email titled “Your Latest Bargaining Update and Strike Vote Info.” It says request by 5 p.m. Monday, Sept. 14, and return by 8 p.m. Tuesday, Sept. 15; ballots are to be emailed Monday. The email does not specify a time zone. Confirm it with our organizer and follow your ballot's return instructions. If a message is missing or you want to change methods, ask before casting another ballot. Do not share personal links.

Can I vote on paid time? Ask our organizer or your steward about the arrangement for your shift before leaving paid duties. No universal paid-time or clock-out rule has been verified.

Can I join and vote? What if membership is pending? Our published FAQ says members in good standing can vote. The briefing said people could join and vote; this vote's written cutoff and verification process still need confirmation. Start your union membership application, then sign and submit it on the next page. Ask Sylvia to verify your voting eligibility, including pending applications or payroll deductions; completing the form alone does not confirm it. The corrected invitation does not require a signed pledge. A pledge is separate from your ballot.

What does yes or no do? Yes gives our bargaining team authority to call a strike; it does not start one immediately. No rejects that authorization on the ballot; it does not by itself accept management's proposal.

What threshold applies, who certifies it, and does yes personally obligate me? Ask Sylvia for the written counting rule, who certifies the result, and guidance on individual obligations or exceptions. These answers have not been verified. The counting rule should state how much support is required and which votes or members count toward it. Do not rely on an unofficial tally. Final written duration/end rules and any further-vote requirement also need verification.

Voting help: Sylvia Sharpe, sharpes@seiu503.org.

Pay proposals and protections

What is the wage difference? In our team's Sept. 11 summary, management proposes a 1.5 percent first-year cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) and 1 percent in year two, with a second-year step freeze, in a four-year term without guaranteed raises in years three and four. Our team proposes 3 percent in each year of a two-year contract and rejects takeaways. These are proposals, not a settled contract.

Did our team agree to freeze steps? What else is at stake? At our briefing, our team said it had not agreed to a step freeze and described preserving full steps both years. Steps apply to eligible workers and differ from the COLA. The Sept. 11 summary also identifies management takeaways involving the guaranteed low-cost health plan, layoffs and contracting out. Ask for current proposal language for your question.

Proposal help: Sylvia Sharpe.

Money and urgent bills

Will I receive pay I already earned? Wages for work performed are separate from pay for strike time. Keep hours/pay records and ask payroll about your cycle or a missing payment; no special strike payday is verified here.

How much strike pay or hardship aid will I get? Union strike pay and emergency hardship aid are separate possible supports. Approved program rules have not been verified. Ask for the approved amount, eligibility, participation/accommodations, private application and payment timing before counting either in your budget. For an urgent bill, give our organizer its due date and a safe callback; do not post financial documents publicly.

Can I get unemployment, and when? Eligible striking workers may receive up to 10 weeks under Oregon's 2026 schedule. OED decides your eligibility and amount. You must be able and available to work and actively seek work; OED currently requires at least one work-seeking activity each week. An unpaid strike week requires less-than-full-time work and earnings below your weekly benefit amount. A separate waiting week may also apply if not already served in your benefit year; payment after 14 days is not guaranteed. File weekly claims, report work earnings even if not yet paid, check for rule changes, and ask OED how union payments affect your claim.

Are retroactive raises or lost-strike-wage payments guaranteed? Must I repay UI? A retroactive contract raise and compensation for wages lost during a strike are different from earned wages, union strike pay, hardship aid and UI. Neither future payment is guaranteed. OED says employer back pay for a strike requires repayment of benefits; ask about the actual payment you receive.

Support help: SEIU 503 Member Assistance, 1-844-503-7348, or Sylvia Sharpe.

Health coverage, leave and tuition

When would my health coverage end? Ask OSU Benefits for your coverage-through date, premium obligations and continuation options in writing, including dependents. No universal strike-related end date is verified.

What if I have surgery or medical leave planned? Check coverage and authorization with your insurer/provider and leave handling with OSU Benefits. This guide is not advice to cancel or postpone care. Keep medical details with authorized case handlers.

Can approved vacation be canceled? What about sick or protected leave? Save the approval and ask for your dates, leave rules and payroll codes in writing. A blanket promise that every approved vacation cannot be changed is not verified. Bring changes to our steward.

When is open enrollment? PEBB open enrollment is Oct. 1–31, 2026, for 2027 benefits. Ask how to enroll if work-system access changes; no extension is established here.

Will employee or dependent tuition benefits continue? Ask about your status, current/next term and deadlines. A briefing expectation is not a guarantee.

Benefits help: OSU Employee Benefits contact, 541-737-2805, and PEBB enrollment information. Add our organizer for union support on employment questions.

Work instructions and rights

What if a manager asks my strike plans? Save the exact question and deadline. Ask our steward how to respond; do not assume every question is illegal. Keep your ballot choice private.

Can I refuse overtime? Save the request and distinguish voluntary overtime, a mandatory assignment and a schedule change. Seek advice before refusing; flag safety or fatigue concerns.

What goes on my timesheet, and what about keys or equipment? Record actual work and approved leave accurately; do not claim strike time as worked. Ask for payroll codes/deadlines and written property-return instructions. Keep records and a handoff receipt.

Will work email stay available? Continued access is not verified. You can voluntarily give our organizer a personal backup contact. Keep only permitted copies of your own records; do not export confidential university or student data.

Do I have to call in? When do I return? Union notice and individual attendance duties are separate. Get instructions for your job and shift before an actual strike and an official return direction afterward. A tentative agreement alone does not direct your return.

What if I face discipline, a layoff or retaliation? Save notices, exact words and dates; contact our steward promptly. Ask about representation if a meeting could lead to discipline. Not every layoff is retaliation.

Contact your steward, Sylvia Sharpe, or SEIU 503 Member Assistance.

Participating and staying informed

When would a strike start, and how long would it last? Authorization, notice and a strike start are distinct. Sept. 28 was discussed as tentative; do not act on it as a start instruction. A final duration/return date is not verified.

What if I cannot stand, travel or work a usual daytime schedule? Tell our organizer your shift/location and the help you need. Ask about approved seated/alternative roles, remote options and benefit rules; do not assume any role qualifies before confirmation.

Do students stop attending classes? Can other workers stop work in support? Our briefing said worker-students are not being asked to stop attending classes. Other workers should get their own guidance before changing work duties; Oregon law limits some picket-line refusals. Ask about approved rallies or personal letters instead of assuming everyone has identical rights.

Where can I bring questions next? Our membership meeting is Thursday, Sept. 17, noon–1 p.m. Pacific, Memorial Union, room 211, or Zoom. Use the meeting page for access. Contact our organizer privately about an individual case.

General union contact hours are weekdays, 8 a.m.–6 p.m., excluding holidays, at 1-844-503-7348. No weekend response time is promised. If an answer appears wrong or out of date, send the question or page title and the correction needed to our organizer without including confidential documents or ballot choices.
