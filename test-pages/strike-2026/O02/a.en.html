O02 — Proposed coworker coverage and response workflow

Internal working plan, Sept. 12–21, 2026. Use our existing Contract Action Team (CAT) and stewards to give each contact a manageable group, a backup and a follow-up time. Contacts listen, help resolve voting barriers and return with answers. All roles, coverage windows and targets below remain proposed and unaccepted. This document creates no member roster or private-case record and establishes no service or funding commitment.

1. Assign a group, owner and backup

At adoption, a local officer should convene the existing CAT/steward coordinators and fill the named-owner and backup fields in a restricted assignment sheet. Each person must accept the group, deadline and backup arrangement; record that acceptance before counting the group as covered. Verify actual shifts and workgroups against the existing authorized roster; do not treat the categories below as a verified map of OSU staffing. Split any assignment a contact cannot realistically complete.

Status choices are unassigned, proposed, accepted, unavailable, completed. A proposed role does not become accepted because its deadline arrives. If no one accepts, show the gap and narrow the public promise. The local officer coordinating adoption is also unassigned until someone accepts.

2. Conduct a conversation and agree on one next step

Start by asking whether now is a workable time. Choose one opening question, then listen: “What part of the proposals matters most to you?” or “What question or practical concern do you have about the vote or a possible strike?” Follow with “Have you seen the voting instructions?” and offer the approved explanation or relevant H01/P02 section. Give the member room to say what would need to be answered or arranged.

Help the member identify a method, time and practical access requirement. Ask whether they want follow-up and which contact method is safe. Questions about household costs, disability, care or skepticism deserve respect. Membership is an ongoing voice in our union. The verified membership application is a usable joining route; completing it requires signing and submitting its next page. It lists 1-844-503-7348 for phone signup if the applicant has no email. Share the route when helpful, then separately verify vote eligibility with the organizer. Do not promise “join now and vote now” before the actual eligibility and verification rules are confirmed.

Close with one concrete agreement: the member will review their own absentee email; the organizer will confirm a destination; the steward will seek a paid-time answer; or the contact will return at an agreed time. Read the follow-up back. If there is no answer yet, say so and identify the next check. Do not mark “resolved” because a link was sent or a referral was made.

Do not ask how the member plans to vote or voted. Do not score loyalty, infer a ballot choice, or copy ballot links. This plan does not need a personal readiness rating. A member may voluntarily identify a practical barrier; record only the action needed, with their consent.

3. Route questions and cases to the right authority

Use three separate records. The public Q&A contains general approved answers. A de-identified internal question queue contains unresolved general questions and source/review work. A restricted case register contains only the contact details needed for an individual follow-up. Neither a public page nor the CAT coverage sheet holds medical records, hardship applications or private workplace evidence.

General-question queue fields and workflow

Record question ID; plain-language question; urgency and actual deadline; affected topic/item; proposed answer owner; factual approver; Spanish reviewer; backup; source/version/date; next check; status; approved public answer location if one exists; and correction/recheck date. Use received → assigned → awaiting authority → drafted → fact-checked → Spanish checked → approved → delivered → recheck due. Only the appropriate subject approver moves an answer to approved.

An editor first checks whether H01–H07/Q01 already holds the answer. If current and approved, send the relevant excerpt or verified link. If it is incomplete, route it to the designated authority and keep the uncertainty visible. A recurring private issue may produce a generalized question only after identifying details are removed.

Urgency and accepted staffing

Prioritize a deadline before the next staffed review: imminent loss of ballot access, a required employer response, an imminent coverage/care problem, and unclear official start/return instructions. General background questions can wait behind these. Flag the deadline itself rather than adding sensitive reasons to the shared dashboard.

Proposed staffed-window goals are to assign deadline-critical requests to a responsible person within the current staffed shift and to give other requests a next-check time within the next accepted staffed day. These are internal planning targets until staff accept them. Do not publish a guaranteed turnaround. A complete handoff needs three recorded steps: the intake coordinator assigns the request, the receiving subject owner acknowledges it, and the member receives a truthful next-check time through their agreed contact method. Forwarding an email alone does not complete the handoff.

Before the vote, record a primary and backup intake window for Sunday preparation and Monday/Tuesday access periods. Before Friday afternoon, record whether Saturday/Sunday follow-up coverage is actually accepted. For every window store start/end/time zone, accepted primary, accepted backup, working contact route, escalation authority and next available staffed window. If both contacts are unavailable, tell the member the verified alternative and next staffed time that is actually known. Do not invent an emergency fund or suggest that waiting for a campaign reply will satisfy an outside deadline.

For aid, the operating checklist is concrete: approved funding/amount or formula; eligibility and accommodation rules; authorized approver; actual private application; necessary documentation; payment method/timing; urgent-case route; reconsideration route; and confirmed coverage/backup. Until these exist, the general guide must identify the gaps and offer case referral, not announce available payments.

4. Keep the minimum record needed for follow-up

The restricted contact register should contain a minimal contact ID or name where necessary, assignment ID, member-approved contact method, permission for follow-up, date reached, next agreed action, assigned owner, due time, status and a short barrier category when volunteered and useful. Examples of category labels are “needs voting location,” “missing ballot email” or “private case referral.” Do not enter a diagnosis, debt amount, ballot choice, pledge-derived assessment, immigration detail or password.

Keep supporting workplace, financial or medical documents only with the authorized case handler through an appropriate restricted channel. The general queue can contain a case reference and a deadline, not the case facts. Aggregate reporting should combine small categories where detail would identify a member. Limit roster access to the assigned contact, coordinator and necessary backup; limit case access to its handler and necessary representation/program personnel. Do not place either record on the public hub.

At adoption, leadership must set the actual authorized storage location and access list before collecting new records. Proposed retention review: on Sept. 22, delete redundant contact copies and notes no longer needed for agreed follow-up; transfer ongoing cases to the authorized case system and record the responsible owner. Keep records that an applicable approved retention rule or active case requires. Set a next review for each retained case; do not accumulate an indefinite campaign dossier. No collection is performed in this draft package.

5. Assign Spanish review and meeting access

Each public item needs an English factual approver, named Spanish reviewer, backup and a shared release deadline. For routine E01–E04/P01/P02 voting content, proposed Spanish review is complete by the same noon Sunday or 6:30 a.m. Monday gate in O01; Tuesday targets follow the actual approved notice. For E06, check both versions before the Thursday 8 a.m. target. For E05/E07/E08 and news, the deadline follows the actual event and accepted review capacity, not a presumed outcome time.

Review critical details side by side: dates/day names, by-versus-after deadlines, request versus return, venue/room, time zone, membership qualifiers, vote versus actual call, notice versus start, tentative agreement versus ratification, current versus superseded instructions, money categories, and exact email/form labels. Use source approval for facts and bilingual review for meaning. Preserve literal email addresses and required English form controls. Agent translation or a fluent-sounding sentence is not human bilingual approval.

When urgent instructions change, issue a short complete English/Spanish core together after review, with the full expanded explanation following when ready. The essential core must still contain the exact affected people, instruction, date/time/time zone and help route. Do not send an English-only start or return instruction with a promise to translate later. If no reviewer is available, escalate that staffing gap immediately and seek an accepted bilingual backup; do not label unchecked copy approved.

For Sept. 17, assign an actual host, entry/question monitor and backup. Verify capacity, audio, entry and language/access arrangements before announcing them. Prepare the same essential facts in writing for members who cannot enter or attend; provide a known question route. A recording/replay is optional and cannot be promised until it exists, sharing is approved and captions/transcript are checked. Do not claim the earlier Zoom problem is fixed from a plan to test it.

6. Correct claims and instructions across channels

For a consequential correction, the editor records: correction ID; actual old text and date; corrected fact; source/version; factual approver; affected IDs/URLs/print versions; English/Spanish reviewer; correction time; distribution owner; and verification evidence. Prioritize wrong place/time/deadline, entitlement, eligibility, or work instruction because members may act on it.

After separate publication authorization, update the maintained answer first, label the change visibly, then notify affected email audiences and worksite contacts. Replace or mark obsolete posters/handouts and update both language versions. Verify the public result; a local edit or passing build is not delivery. Preserve historical news with a dated correction/current-status link where needed. Track whether contacts with affected members received the corrected instruction, not merely whether an editor clicked send.

For a management message, preserve the exact dated claim privately, identify what our union agrees with, disputes or needs clarified, gather the actual proposal/contract/agency evidence, and obtain the right review. An approved general response should explain the claim, our response, evidence, remaining uncertainty and requested member action. Do not exaggerate the message or label it false/unlawful without support. Member identities and case details stay private.

Every deliverable entry needs ID, responsible named editor, subject approver, backup, EN/ES review status, due time, intended destination, external authorization status and readback/delivery evidence. Use separate states for draft complete, approved, released and verified delivered. This package only creates drafts.

Proposed process target: every accepted assignment has a backup and next check, every reported deadline-critical barrier has a receiving owner, and every essential release has matched English/Spanish instructions. These are goals to adopt and staff, not existing results. There is no invented turnout, persuasion or financial-readiness target. A member who declines a conversation is not a failure or an inferred no vote.
