What our authorization vote means

Information checked Saturday, Sept. 12, 2026.

A yes vote gives our bargaining team authority to call a strike. It does not tell members to stop work immediately. Read the ballot, ask the questions you need answered and take part in our union's decision.

What do yes and no mean?

Our union's strike-pledge FAQ explains that the authorization vote gives our bargaining team authority to call a strike. Our team's stated aim is to build the power to win a fair contract.

A no vote is a vote against granting that authorization on this ballot. It is not, by itself, a vote to accept management's contract proposal. The exact ballot wording and written explanation of the next steps should guide your decision; ask the election administrator through our organizer if either is unclear.

Signing a strike pledge shows support. Casting a ballot makes your choice in this vote. An official strike call provides work-action instructions. These are separate steps.

Who can vote? Can I join first?

The published FAQ says members in good standing can vote. The corrected voting invitation includes members whether or not they signed the pledge.

At our member briefing, speakers said nonmembers could join and vote. You can start your union membership application now. The form says your application is not complete until you sign and submit it on the next page.

We have not verified this vote's written membership cutoff or eligibility-verification process. Contact Sylvia Sharpe at sharpes@seiu503.org to confirm your voting eligibility, including if your application or payroll deduction is pending. Completing an application does not by itself confirm eligibility for this vote; a payroll deduction alone does not settle your status either.

What number is needed, and who certifies the result?

Ask our organizer for the written counting rule: how much support is required, which votes or members count toward that threshold, and who certifies the result. Those rules have not been verified in the sources checked. At the briefing, the answer emphasized assessing support rather than a “magic number.” That does not supply the written counting rule.

Our organizer can request the rule from the election administrator. After voting, look for the officially certified result and its explanation. Unofficial tallies do not establish the result. We cannot promise a certification date from the information checked.

Does yes personally obligate me to strike?

Our campaign asks members to stand together, and your questions about participation deserve a clear answer. We have not verified complete written guidance on individual legal or internal union obligations, consequences or exceptions. Ask our union representative how the rules apply to your circumstances. You can discuss a participation barrier privately without disclosing your ballot choice.

Is the strike for a fixed time? Who starts and ends it?

Authorization gives our bargaining team authority to call a strike, but we have not verified final written rules for its duration, ending the action or any further member vote. A goal such as winning a fair agreement does not establish a return date.

Oregon's public-sector process includes bargaining requirements and notice before a lawful strike by a strike-permitted unit. Authorization alone does not prove those requirements have been completed. Follow our union's actual start and return instructions when issued. Sept. 28 was discussed as tentative; it is not a verified start instruction.

Take the next step

Make your voting plan using the confirmed Monday daytime voting details. Send unresolved eligibility or ballot-rule questions to Sylvia Sharpe. General help is also available through SEIU 503's contact page or 1-844-503-7348 during published weekday hours, 8 a.m.–6 p.m., excluding holidays.
