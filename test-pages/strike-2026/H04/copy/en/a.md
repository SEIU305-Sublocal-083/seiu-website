# Plan for money during a possible strike

Information checked Saturday, Sept. 12, 2026.

**Build your household plan around money you can confirm, and list possible support separately.** Questions about rent, food, medicine and family responsibilities belong in our strike preparation. You can ask for help without explaining or defending your vote.

### Keep six kinds of money separate

| Type | What it means for your plan |
|---|---|
| **1. Wages already earned** | Pay for work you already performed. Keep your hours and pay records. Ask payroll about your own pay cycle or a missing payment; this is separate from pay for time on strike. |
| **2. Union strike pay** | A possible union-funded benefit with its own rules. An approved amount, eligibility, participation requirements and payment schedule for this action have not been verified in the sources checked. A yes vote does not establish benefit eligibility. |
| **3. Emergency hardship aid** | Possible assistance for urgent need. We have not verified an approved program's rules, administrator, application or payment timing. Ask what help is currently available before counting it as income. |
| **4. Oregon Unemployment Insurance (UI)** | A state benefit that may be available to eligible striking workers. OED decides individual eligibility and amount; weekly requirements apply. |
| **5. Retroactive contract wage increases** | A later agreement could apply a new wage rate to an earlier period. Whether this happens, for which work and when it is paid depends on the actual agreement. It is not guaranteed. |
| **6. Compensation for strike-related lost wages** | Money specifically covering wages lost during a strike, if later negotiated or awarded. Our briefing discussed seeking it; that is not a promise it will be won. It can affect UI repayment. |

Do not add all six together as guaranteed wage replacement.

### Make a short, private household plan

1. List essential bills and due dates for the next several weeks.
2. List funds already available and payments you can verify. Put unapproved assistance and possible future compensation in a separate column.
3. Identify the first gap: the amount needed, the due date and a safe way to contact you. You do not need to put private financial or medical details in a public question or group reply.
4. Ask our organizer who can handle that need privately and which current programs accept applications. Keep copies of any approved benefit rules and application confirmation.

For hardship help, ask:

- What amount is approved, and who qualifies?
- What participation or accommodation rules apply?
- Who reviews requests, what documents are needed, and how are they kept private?
- When could payment arrive? What happens if a request is denied or a bill is due first?

### What Oregon UI currently requires

Oregon's current guidance allows **up to 10 weeks of benefits for eligible striking workers under the 2026 schedule**. That is a limit, not a guarantee. Use OED's [striking-worker instructions and benefit-calculator link](https://unemployment.oregon.gov/strikes) for the application route and an estimate.

When you are out of work because of a strike, follow OED's application or restart instructions. The guidance says to indicate that you still work for the employer and select **“strike or lockout”** as the separation reason. Continue filing the required weekly claims.

- You must be able to work, available for work and actively seeking work. Current guidance requires **at least one work-seeking activity each week**; keep a record and check for changes.
- A qualifying **unpaid strike week** comes first. To receive credit for that week, you must work less than full time and earn less than your weekly benefit amount.
- A separate regular **waiting week** may also be required if you have not already served it in your benefit year. These weeks and claim review mean there is no promised payment after 14 days.
- Report earnings for work performed in each claimed week, including other jobs, even if payment has not arrived. School-break rules can affect some claims.

These are OED's rules, and OED decides how they apply to you. Ask OED specifically how to report union strike pay or hardship aid; the strike page does not settle that question. Do not assume either payment can be omitted from a claim. [Read OED's current guidance](https://unemployment.oregon.gov/strikes).

### Could I have to repay unemployment benefits?

**Yes. OED says benefits must be repaid if an employer provides back pay for a strike.** Keep any agreement, award and payment statement and promptly ask OED how to handle it. A retroactive raise on work you performed is a different category from compensation for strike-lost wages; ask how the actual payment affects your claim. [OED's back-pay explanation](https://unemployment.oregon.gov/strikes).
