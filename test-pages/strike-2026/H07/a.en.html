How a strike would work and how to prepare

Information checked Saturday, Sept. 12, 2026.

For now, prepare your questions and participation plan. Our authorization vote is not an instruction to walk out. A strike is a collective decision with real effects on our work and households. We need clear instructions and ways to participate that account for members' schedules and access needs.

Understand the sequence

  1. Authorization: Members decide whether to give our bargaining team authority to call a strike.
  2. Legal process and notice: Oregon's required public-sector bargaining process and notice requirements are separate from our internal vote.
  3. Official strike call: Our union provides the actual start and work instructions for covered workers. A discussed date or an unofficial message is not that instruction.
  4. Strike and bargaining: A work stoppage means withholding covered work. Picketing is a coordinated activity with its own locations, shifts and instructions. Bargaining can continue.
  5. Official next steps and return: Follow the verified instructions for any agreement, changed timeline and return to work. A tentative agreement is not a ratified contract or an automatic return order.

Read SEIU 503's authorization explanation and Oregon ERB's bargaining process. No finalized strike duration or start time has been verified for this guide. Sept. 28 remains a tentative date discussed at the briefing, not a start instruction.

Plan for your location, shift and access needs

Tell our organizer what you would need to participate. You can raise a barrier without sharing a diagnosis or your ballot choice. Useful questions include:

  • Which instructions apply to my overnight, early-morning, weekend or remote shift?
  • Where is the approved picket location, and how do I check in?
  • Is there a seated role, accessible route, rest area or another approved way to help?
  • What options exist for distant worksites and members who cannot travel?
  • What transportation, food, training or language support is actually arranged?
  • If strike pay is available, which participation and accommodation rules apply?

Do not assume an alternative role qualifies for a benefit until the program administrator confirms it. Ask for an answer before agreeing to an assignment you cannot safely perform.

If a strike is officially called

Read the dated instructions before your affected shift. Confirm the start time, which work is covered, any attendance-reporting steps, your picket or other assignment, and the help route. If any part does not fit your schedule or access needs, seek clarification before the start.

At an approved picket, follow the coordinator's instructions, keep emergency access clear and use peaceful conduct. Do not threaten, shame or block people. Ask how to report an incident and where to get immediate help; preparations for food, weather, transportation and accessibility should match your assignment.

What if I am also a student?

At our briefing, speakers said workers who are also students are not being asked to stop attending classes. Ask separately about any concern involving your job, class access, tuition or university systems. Student enrollment and covered employment are different roles.

Students and coworkers outside our bargaining unit can ask about approved rallies or personal letters of support. They should get guidance for their own situation before changing work duties. Oregon law has specific limits on other public employees refusing to cross a picket line; do not assume everyone on campus has the same strike rights. See ORS 243.732.

If the timeline changes or a strike lasts longer

Use our union's newest dated instructions and update your household and shift plans. If an actual strike continues, revisit essential bills, approved assistance, health coverage for the next month and any weekly unemployment obligations. Tell our organizer when an earlier participation plan no longer works; needing a different role or support is a reason to ask for help.

A rumor, a predicted end date or news of a tentative agreement is not a substitute for official return instructions. Confirm your return time, shift and any attendance or property steps.
