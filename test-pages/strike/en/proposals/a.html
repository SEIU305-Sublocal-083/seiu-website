What we are bargaining for

Based on our bargaining team's Friday, Sept. 11, 2026 update. Information checked Sept. 12.

Our campaign is about pay, affordable health coverage and protection for our work. Our bargaining team is seeking 3 percent wage increases in each year of a two-year contract and rejecting takeaways. Management's proposal would provide smaller wage increases over a longer contract and weaken protections, according to our team's Sept. 11 summary.

These are bargaining positions, not a settled contract. Use this comparison to prepare for the vote, and ask our team about details that affect your job.

First-year cost-of-living adjustment 1.5 percent 3 percent
Second-year cost-of-living adjustment 1 percent 3 percent

Layoff and contracting-out protections Proposed takeaways from existing protections Reject those takeaways

A cost-of-living adjustment and a step are different

A cost-of-living adjustment, or COLA, changes the wage schedule. A step increase moves an eligible worker to a higher step on that schedule. Not every worker is eligible for a step in every year. Ask how your current step and eligibility affect your pay before adding a step increase to your COLA.

At the member briefing, our team clarified that it had not agreed to a step freeze and described its proposal as preserving full steps in both years. That detail comes from the briefing; the current written proposal is the place to confirm the exact terms.

“No guaranteed raises in years three and four” describes the guarantee missing from management's proposed four-year term. It does not mean we can predict every future wage decision.

Why the protections matter

Affordable coverage affects what families can spend on other needs. Keeping a guaranteed low-cost option is a bargaining issue; it is separate from whether your individual coverage continues during a strike.

Contracting out means assigning work to an outside contractor. Layoff protections can shape how reductions affect jobs and workers. Bumping rights can allow movement into another position during a layoff, subject to contract requirements; they are not a guarantee that every worker can take another position. Exact changes require the actual proposal language.

Together, pay and these protections affect our ability to stay in our jobs and support students. Our bargaining team is asking for strike authorization to strengthen our campaign for a fair agreement. Authorization does not itself start a strike or guarantee a particular settlement.
