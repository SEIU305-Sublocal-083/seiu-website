01About Our StrikeWhat is happening, why we are preparing and how we will know what comes next.+

What is a strike?

A strike is workers stopping work together to press for better pay or working conditions. The aim is to move the employer toward an agreement. A picket line is where workers gather to make that action visible.

[9]

Learn why workers strike

Why are we preparing to strike now?

We have not reached an agreement. After Aug. 5–6 mediation, our team declared an impasse, meaning the disagreement remained unresolved. In results announced Sept. 17, 97 percent of members covered by the Higher Education contract who voted authorized a strike. That gave our union permission to call one. Local 083 delivered notice to OSU leadership Sept. 18.

The vote included members covered by that contract beyond OSU. Talks continue.

[1] [7] [8]

Read the notice and next steps

What do we want from a fair contract?

Wages that keep up with everyday costs, health coverage we can afford and protection for our jobs. When wages fall behind prices, our paychecks buy less. When health coverage costs more, less is left for rent, groceries and family needs.

Our notice identifies unresolved issues in pay, benefits, insurance, hiring outside contractors to do our work, layoffs and contract length. These terms affect whether we can afford to stay in the jobs that keep OSU running. Read the dated updates for the latest proposals.

[1]

Read what remains unresolved

Is the strike definitely happening? When would it start?

It could still be avoided through an agreement. Our notice identifies Monday, Sept. 28, 2026, at 6 a.m. Pacific as the intended start. Bargaining is scheduled for Sept. 24. Prepare for that start and follow written union updates for any change.

[1]

How will we know when to return to work?

Our union will share written return-to-work instructions and explain how members can review and vote on any proposed agreement. Keep following our updates, and contact us if you need help understanding the next step.

How can standing together help us win a fair contract?

A strike is workers stopping work together. Workers may choose to strike when the offered terms are unacceptable and acting together could help win a fair agreement. That decision calls for clear facts, honest discussion and practical preparation.

Concern about a strike is reasonable.

Lost pay, care responsibilities, student disruption and uncertainty are real. Our preparation should help members work through those concerns with facts, respect and concrete support.

The practical case

Why stopping work can change negotiations

OSU relies on classified workers to keep its operations running. Withholding that work can make an unresolved dispute more costly and visible, increasing pressure for an agreement. How much pressure an action creates depends on participation, the work affected, public support and management’s response.

A strike does not guarantee a particular result. Our bargaining team still needs to negotiate the proposed agreement’s language, wages and conditions. Members also need a clear way to understand and evaluate that agreement.

A strike has a legal process

Oregon’s public employee bargaining law sets conditions for lawful strikes, including completion of the required bargaining steps, an end to applicable contractual no-strike restrictions and required notice. Some public employee groups cannot strike. A general statement that workers have a right to strike is not permission to walk out at any time. [43]

Use our Oregon strike-rights guide for the process, and the strike hub for our current instructions. Ask a steward about your employment category, work location or individual concern before relying on a rule meant for someone else.

An informed decision

Questions our members should be able to answer

Bring these questions to a membership meeting, a steward or our bargaining team. A concern about rent, a proposal or a work assignment deserves a specific answer.

What are we trying to win?

Ask which issues remain unresolved, what each side proposes and what the differences mean for your pay and working conditions. Use the dated proposals and updates linked from our bargaining hub; a number recalled from a meeting may refer to an earlier offer.

What has happened in bargaining?

Ask what changed in the latest session, whether another session is scheduled and what would make an agreement acceptable. Read current updates alongside the legal process rather than assuming that one step guarantees a settlement.

What does my household need?

Review expected missed wages, bill dates, insurance questions and the timing of any support. Our pay and benefits guide separates strike benefits from unemployment insurance and explains taxes. Do not build a budget on a payment that has not been confirmed.

How can I participate?

Ask about your assigned location, check-in, accessibility, transportation and care obligations. Tell an organizer about a barrier early so our union can discuss options and confirm any applicable benefit requirements.

How will decisions reach members?

Know where to find official updates and how to ask a question. A pledge, an authorization vote, a strike notice and a tentative agreement are different events. Use the current announcement to understand what action members are being asked to take.

What happens when an agreement is reached?

Ask how members will receive the proposed terms, what voting process applies and who will communicate return-to-work instructions. Do not infer a return date from an informal report that negotiations are going well.

Will I still get paid on Sept. 30?
Your next payday

Will I still get paid on Sept. 30?

Yes. OSU’s payroll calendar lists Wednesday, Sept. 30, as payday. You are still owed the wages you have earned on the regular pay schedule. [60] [61]

Unpaid strike hours are not paid work time. A pay adjustment may appear in that paycheck or a later payroll correction, depending on your pay period and processing. The payday is confirmed; your own hours and deductions determine the amount.

Bring pay-statement or time-sheet questions to our union. We can help check the dates covered, estimate a reduction for unpaid hours and follow up on anything that does not look right.

Email our union for help with pay and benefits

Will we receive strike pay, and how much?

Our Sept. 15 update reports approval of $80 per day, up to $400 per week, for workers who are striking and attend a picket line. These are the announced amounts before any personal tax obligation. [23]

How do I enroll, and when should I expect payment?

Our executive team can help you register, explain how attendance is recorded and share payment instructions as they are confirmed. Keep your participation records, and let us know if a bill is due before your payment so we can help you look at support options.

[2]

Request strike-pay instructions

What help is there if I still cannot pay my bills?

The same update reports a $500,000 fund shared by Higher Education and Marion County, with initial help of up to $1,000 per eligible member, limited to missed pay. Members requesting assistance must have been active strikers and may submit a bill for support. Assistance requires review; it is not an automatic payment. [23]

Is strike pay taxable?

Is strike pay taxable?

Strike pay is taxable income. IRS Publication 525 generally treats union strike and lockout benefits as income. Its narrow exception for a payment established as a gift does not make ordinary strike pay tax-free. Keep payment records and set aside money for taxes as appropriate for your household. [21]

Report taxable strike pay even if no tax form arrives. Our union can help with payment records and documentation; a tax preparer can help you report the payments for your household. [22]

Hardship assistance needs a separate answer. We have not verified the tax treatment of each kind of hardship payment. Do not assume that a bill paid on your behalf is automatically tax-free; ask for written program guidance. The IRS generally counts income paid to someone else on your behalf as your income unless an exclusion applies. [22]
Can I take paid leave instead, and what goes on my time sheet?
What should I put on my time sheet?

Keep a record of your work, approved leave and strike hours. Our stewards can help you find the correct written timekeeping instructions for your job and understand the submission deadline. Let us know if you need help with your payroll system or a pay adjustment.

Will I keep my health coverage?
Will my health coverage continue?

Yes. If you are enrolled as a benefits-eligible classified employee and have at least 80 hours of work or paid leave in September, your coverage continues through Oct. 31. Coverage is earned for the following calendar month; it does not stop on the first day of a strike. [33]

Count hours worked and paid leave in September toward the 80-hour requirement. Unpaid strike hours do not count. For example, 80 eligible hours earned before Sept. 28 meet that monthly hours requirement for October coverage.

Working part-time, sharing a position or already taking unpaid leave? Reach out to our union. We can help add up your eligible hours, review your appointment and premium share, and work through the rules that apply to you.

Let’s do the math together. Have your schedule and the total hours worked or taken as paid leave handy. We will use your actual appointment and coverage rules. Email our union for help with pay and benefits.
What if I may have fewer than 80 eligible hours?

Contact our union so we can help review your eligibility. Some employees have continued coverage under other rules, such as an Affordable Care Act stability period. We can help find the rule that applies to your appointment. [63]

If your plan does require continuation coverage, we can help you understand the notice, premium and deadline. PEBB’s COBRA option requires an election and payment; it is not automatic. [28]

Keep open enrollment on your calendar: Oct. 1–31, 2026, for 2027 PEBB benefits. Reach out to us if you need help finding the enrollment steps or accessing your account. [34]

Is unemployment another source of support?
Oregon unemployment insurance

Apply when your hours fall, and keep filing weekly.

Prepare a Frances Online account now. When you become unemployed or your hours are reduced, apply or restart a claim and follow Oregon Employment Department (OED) instructions. File a weekly claim for each week you seek benefits, including while a decision is pending. Answer every question accurately. [24]

  1. Identify the strike correctly. OED directs you to indicate you are still working for the employer involved in the strike and select “strike or lockout.” [25]
  2. Meet weekly requirements. Be able and available to work; currently, striking claimants must complete and record at least one work-seeking activity weekly. Recheck OED guidance because this requirement may change. [25]
  3. Report hours and gross earnings for the week worked. Include other jobs and work before the strike began, even if the paycheck arrives later. Ask OED how to report strike pay or hardship assistance; do not assume those payments are ignored or automatically deducted. [26]

Unemployment benefits are taxable, too

Oregon unemployment benefits are subject to federal and state income tax. OED offers withholding choices and provides a Form 1099-G. Withholding is a payment toward your tax bill, not a determination of the final amount you owe. [27]

Can I receive more food benefits (SNAP)?
Will my SNAP food benefits continue?

Taking part in a strike does not automatically end an eligible household’s SNAP benefits. Under the striker rules, your household must have been eligible immediately before the strike and continue to meet the program’s requirements. A drop in income from striking alone does not increase the benefit. Exceptions to the striker rules may apply. [29]

Our union can help you understand reporting requirements, prepare questions for the Oregon Department of Human Services (ODHS) and find food support. ODHS makes eligibility decisions. Keep up with required reports and renewal deadlines. For help with groceries now, use our food and housing guide.

You can apply through Oregon ONE or call 800-699-9075. Each assistance program has its own rules; we can help you find the right next step. [30]

How could unpaid time affect my retirement?
Will I still earn retirement service credit?

Unpaid strike time does not automatically mean losing a month of PERS service credit. Under the Public Employees Retirement System’s general rule, unpaid leave is reported when it reaches 11 business days in a calendar month. Fewer unpaid days do not trigger that monthly leave-without-pay rule; usual service-credit eligibility still applies. Count any other unpaid leave in the same month, too. [31]

Retirement contributions are based on eligible pay, so a lower paycheck can mean a smaller contribution. [62] If you work part-time, have another unpaid absence, work a nonstandard schedule or are close to retirement, our union can help review your dates and questions with you.

What should I check before a missed paycheck?

  • What strike support or help with groceries would be useful?
  • Who can help with a form, time sheet or benefit notice?

Work through the readiness checklist

Start here

Your strike-readiness checklist

You can prepare now, even while you still have questions about the intended strike. Start with your household needs, important records and access to union updates.

Before any strike
  • Save our union’s contact information outside your work account.
  • Download recent pay statements, leave balances and benefit documents you are allowed to keep.
  • Check your Sept. 30 payday and upcoming bills. Ask our union for help estimating any change in pay.
  • Check your September hours for October health coverage. We can help with the calculation and with food-benefit questions.
Before the intended start
  • Confirm the date, time, covered bargaining unit and instructions through official updates from our union.
  • Use personal devices and accounts for strike activity, and protect confidential work information.
  • If you expect to claim unemployment, follow Oregon Employment Department instructions to apply or restart promptly and keep filing weekly claims. Waiting weeks still require eligibility. [14]
  • Keep copies of management messages and your own factual notes.
During a strike
  • Follow peaceful picket and safety instructions from our strike leaders.
  • Report hours and earnings accurately to benefit programs.
  • Talk with coworkers respectfully. Answer questions with facts, make space for concerns and never use threats or shame.
  • Send media or legal questions to the contacts named by our union.
Make your plan concrete

Five conversations to have before Sept. 28

  1. With your household: list rent or mortgage, utilities, groceries, care costs and minimum debt payments. Separate money already available from benefits that require an application or have unconfirmed payment dates. Strike pay is taxable; the approved weekly maximum is not a take-home guarantee. [12] [13]
  2. With our organizer or a steward: confirm the covered bargaining unit, plans for remote workers, picket assignment, check-in process and accessible alternatives. Ask whether an alternative assignment qualifies for strike pay before including it in your budget. The published announcement specifies striking workers who attend a picket line. [12]
  3. With our union about health coverage: if you are unsure about your hours or work part-time, we can help count the work and paid-leave hours that earn next month’s coverage. Bring your schedule and any coverage question; ask us how to share personal information privately. [15]
  4. With a steward about your paycheck: Sept. 30 remains OSU’s scheduled payday. We can help you check the pay period, understand any adjustment for unpaid time and find the timekeeping instructions for your job. Keep your own record of hours. [60]
  5. With a coworker: share the latest written update, exchange personal contact information by consent and make room for questions. Ask about a ride, food, child care or an accessible role without promising a service that has not been arranged.

Keep your information within reach

Save our steward contact, our executive-team contact and these guides on a personal device. Keep only records you are entitled to possess; protect student, coworker and other confidential workplace information.

For benefits, track application dates, confirmation numbers, any documents requested and each program’s reporting instructions. Ask the agency how to report a payment rather than assuming strike pay, hardship aid and wages all count the same way.

Unsure what a message means? Send it to our union with the date, your question and any deadline. We can help interpret it and work with the appropriate office when an individual determination or correction is needed.

What does support from other unions mean?

Solidarity means workers choosing to back each other up across workplaces and unions. Other unions offered support on their own. Local 083 did not ask them to act, and we welcomed their support. Each union decides what its members will do, so support does not mean every worker or every delivery will be affected.

Make a household plan

Questions to ask before bills come due

  1. Name the immediate gap. List food, rent, medications, transportation and care costs, with due dates. Separate money already available from payments you have applied for.
  2. Ask the provider what happens next. Find out whether there is a waitlist, an appointment or a document deadline. Write down the date, contact and next step.
  3. Plan for access. Ask about language support, disability access, transportation, delivery and whether someone can pick up food for your household.
  4. Tell our union what would help. Contact a steward or our executive team about a ride, an accessible participation option or a hardship question. Ask what is confirmed before counting on an arrangement.

How does this fit with strike pay?

Community services, union strike benefits and unemployment insurance are separate programs. Read our strike pay and benefits guide for the taxable-income correction, application requirements and timing. Do not assume an award from one program establishes eligibility for another.

Payday and benefit clarification · sources 60–63

Checked Sept. 22, 2026. The Sept. 30 payday comes from OSU’s calendar. Applying the monthly coverage rule to September gives October coverage for eligible enrolled classified employees who meet the requirement. Individual payroll adjustments, appointment exceptions and program decisions need the member’s own records; our union can help with that review.
