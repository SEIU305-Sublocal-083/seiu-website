01 What is happening, and why?

What is a strike?

A strike is workers stopping work together to press for better pay or working conditions. The aim is to move the employer toward an agreement. A picket line is where workers gather to make that action visible.

[9]

Learn why workers strike

Why are we preparing to strike now?

We have not reached an agreement. After Aug. 5–6 mediation, our team declared an impasse, meaning the disagreement remained unresolved. In results announced Sept. 17, 97% of members covered by the Higher Education contract who voted authorized a strike. That gave our union permission to call one. Local 083 delivered notice to OSU leadership Sept. 18.

The vote included members covered by that contract beyond OSU. Talks continue.

[1] [7] [8]

Read the notice and next steps

What do we want from a fair contract?

Wages that keep up with everyday costs, health coverage we can afford and protection for our jobs. When wages fall behind prices, our paychecks buy less. When health coverage costs more, less is left for rent, groceries and family needs.

Our notice identifies unresolved issues in pay, benefits, insurance, hiring outside contractors to do our work, layoffs and contract length. These terms affect whether we can afford to stay in the jobs that keep OSU running. Read the dated updates for the latest proposals.

[1]

Read what remains unresolved

Is the strike definitely happening? When would it start?

It could still be avoided through an agreement. Our notice identifies Monday, Sept. 28, 2026, at 6 a.m. Pacific as the intended start. Bargaining is scheduled for Sept. 24. Prepare for that start and follow written union updates for any change.

[1]

Check the latest bargaining updates

02 Understand what taking part means

Am I one of the workers being asked to strike?

Our notice covers SEIU-represented classified staff in the Higher Education contract who are legally permitted to strike. Other OSU workers may have different contracts and rules. If you are unsure whether your job is covered, send your job title and department to our stewards. A steward is a union representative who helps with workplace questions.

[1] [5]

Ask about your position

How can I get ready without knowing every detail yet?

Save union contacts outside your work account. List essential bills and due dates, and discuss transportation, medications and care with your household. Separate money you have from support with unknown payment dates. Tell our union early about a barrier to taking part.

Work through the readiness checklist

Where will I picket, and what does checking in mean?

Follow the written picket schedule from our strike leadership for your location, shift and check-in instructions. Check-in is how your attendance is recorded; attending a picket line is a published condition for strike pay. If you have not received instructions, contact our executive team.

[2]

Request the schedule and check-in instructions

What if a disability or responsibility keeps me from picketing?

Explain the barrier to our stewards and ask what arrangements are available. If an alternative is proposed, confirm the assignment and whether it qualifies for strike pay. The published announcement requires picket attendance, so do not assume an alternative qualifies.

[2]

Discuss participation support

What should I do if my manager asks about my plans or pressures me?

Bring the exact question or instruction to a steward. Save emails or note what was said, when and by whom, including any deadline. A steward can explain your options. If a meeting could lead to discipline, ask about union representation.

03 Plan for your income and benefits

Will we receive strike pay, and how much?

Our union approved $80 a day, up to $400 a week, for workers who strike and attend a picket line. Strike pay is union support toward the wages you miss. Confirm the registration and attendance requirements before counting on a payment.

Is strike pay taxable?

Strike pay is taxable income. Keep payment records and plan for taxes, even if you receive no tax form.

[2] [3]

Understand strike pay and benefits

How do I enroll, and when should I expect payment?

The published announcement does not give a first-payment date or a complete signup process. Ask our executive team how to register, how attendance will be recorded and when to expect payment. Keep your participation records and plan for bills that may come due first.

[2]

Request strike-pay instructions

What help is there if I still cannot pay my bills?

Our union has announced a separate hardship fund for active strikers struggling with bills. Initial assistance is up to $1,000 and cannot exceed missed pay. Members may submit a bill for help. Ask our executive team about the application process and how to provide documents privately.

[2]

Ask how hardship assistance works

Is unemployment another source of support?

Potentially. Oregon allows benefits for some striking workers, but the Oregon Employment Department decides each claim. Waiting requirements and weekly eligibility apply, and payment is not guaranteed. Follow its instructions for applying and filing weekly claims, and ask how to report your earnings and other payments.

[4]

Read Oregon’s guidance for striking workers

Will I keep my health coverage?

You will need to confirm how unpaid strike time would affect your own coverage with OSU Benefits. Get coverage dates, your share of premiums and any options for keeping coverage in writing. Mention upcoming treatment or prescriptions so you can plan ahead.

[6]

Get your coverage questions answered

Can I take paid leave instead, and what goes on my time sheet?

Do not assume vacation or sick leave can cover strike time. Discuss planned leave with a steward, and get written timekeeping instructions from OSU Payroll for your employee group. Record actual work, approved leave and strike absences accurately. Ask before changing a leave plan or alternating between working and striking.

Ask about your leave and timekeeping

04 Stay connected and support each other

How will we know when to return to work?

Our union will communicate return-to-work instructions through written updates. The notice does not set a strike length. Follow those instructions rather than an informal report that a deal is close, and look for information about reviewing and voting on any proposed agreement.

[1]

Follow bargaining updates

What does support from other unions mean?

Solidarity means workers choosing to back each other up across workplaces and unions. Other unions offered support on their own. Local 083 did not ask them to act, and we welcomed their support. Each union decides what its members will do, so support does not mean every worker or every delivery will be affected.

[10]

Find help with food or bills

How can family, students and neighbors help?

Share our current updates and explain why this contract matters to the people who keep OSU running. If you can offer food, transportation, supplies or other help, contact our executive team. We can connect offers with confirmed needs and provide current instructions.
